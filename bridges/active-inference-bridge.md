## What Active Inference is

Active Inference is a framework for understanding how biological (and artificial) agents perceive, act, and learn. It is derived from the **Free Energy Principle**: living systems act to minimize their free energy — a measure of surprise, or the gap between what is expected and what is encountered.

The framework rests on several interlocking concepts:

**Generative model** — The agent maintains an internal probabilistic model of the world that generates predictions about expected sensory inputs. The model is what persists; it is the prior structure that makes recognition and anticipation possible.

**Prediction error** — The signal produced when the world does not match the model's predictions. Prediction error is the primary driver of learning and action. Both perception and action work to reduce it: perception updates the model, action changes the world.

**Precision weighting** — Not all prediction errors are weighted equally. Precision is the agent's confidence in a prediction. High precision means strong attention to that signal. Precision weighting governs what the agent treats as important.

**Expected free energy** — Future-oriented prediction that drives action selection. It has two components: epistemic (reducing uncertainty, exploration) and pragmatic (achieving expected outcomes, exploitation). The interplay between these explains the tension between curiosity and commitment.

**Markov blankets** — A formal partition separating a system's internal states from external states, with sensory and active states as the boundary. The Markov blanket defines where Contact happens — the interface between an agent and its environment.

**Nested Markov blankets** — Active Inference extends naturally to multiple scales. Cells have Markov blankets; organisms do; groups and institutions do. Each scale has its own generative model, its own prediction error, its own active boundary. Systems nest within systems.

## Why Active Inference is relevant to Triadic Integrity v2

Active Inference is the first Bridge candidate for v2 because it directly tests several of v2's core structural claims:

1. **The scale-recursive structure of Layer 2** — Active Inference independently arrives at nested, hierarchical systems with the same logical structure as TIF's recursive scales. Two very different starting points converging on similar architecture is evidence that something real is being described.

2. **The Contact–Continuity relationship** — Active Inference gives a formal account of how encounter (prediction error) and pattern (generative model) interact — which is precisely the ●–■ Contact–Continuity dyad. This is the most robust point of translation between the two frameworks.

3. **The multi-scale diagnostic** — Active Inference's extension to social and collective systems (collective active inference, group generative models) provides independent grounding for TIF's claim that the same mechanics recur at Agent, Relation, and Field Scale.

4. **Force tensions as expected free energy** — The interplay between epistemic drive (curiosity, novelty) and pragmatic drive (consolidation, security) in Active Inference provides a formal analog to the tension between Qualitative Force (⟶★) and Continuity Force (⟶■) in TIF.

The goal of this Bridge is not to claim that TIF is derived from Active Inference, or that Active Inference validates TIF. The goal is to identify where translation is productive, where it is only loose, and where it breaks down.

## Resonances

### ● Contact ↔ Prediction error / Markov blanket interface

**Strength: Good**

Contact (●) in TIF names encounter, friction, and reality-testing — the point at which something meets what is not simply under its control.

In Active Inference, prediction error is precisely this signal: the mismatch between what the generative model expected and what the world produced. The Markov blanket formalizes *where* Contact occurs — the sensory and active interface between system and environment.

Both frameworks treat Contact as primary: experience is not just inward processing; something resists, responds, or deviates from expectation. The formal structure of prediction error and the structural concept of Contact are doing closely related work.

### ■ Continuity ↔ Generative model / Prior beliefs

**Strength: Good**

Continuity (■) in TIF names pattern over time — what persists, enables recognition, and carries form forward.

In Active Inference, the generative model is exactly what persists across encounters. It is the prior belief structure that makes recognition and anticipation possible. Without it, each moment of Contact would be uninterpretable. The generative model is Continuity formalized.

The relationship is recursive in both frameworks: Contact/prediction error updates the model/Continuity, and the model/Continuity shapes what counts as Contact/prediction error in the next moment.

### ★ Quality ↔ Precision weighting

**Strength: Loose**

Quality (★) in TIF is felt salience — the vividness, charge, or qualitative character that marks something as mattering before interpretation is complete.

In Active Inference, precision weighting determines how strongly the agent attends to a given signal. High precision = high effective weight = something treated as important.

The resonance is real but imprecise. Precision is a statistical quantity (a measure of confidence in a distribution). Quality is phenomenological (the felt character of mattering). Precision can model *when* something is attended to; it cannot model *what it is like* for something to matter. The translation is useful for structural analysis but should not be read as an equivalence.

### The Three Forces ↔ Expected free energy components

**Strength: Moderate — loose mapping, not equivalence**

Active Inference's expected free energy has two primary components:
- **Epistemic value**: the drive to reduce uncertainty — curiosity, exploration, seeking new information.
- **Pragmatic value**: the drive to achieve expected outcomes — exploitation, consolidation, keeping the world matching prior expectations.

TIF's Three Forces are:
- **Qualitative Force (⟶★)**: What is trying to come alive, be expressed, or be noticed — the pull toward what is vivid, meaningful, or distinctive.
- **Contact Force (⟶●)**: The force of actual engagement — what is being demanded by real encounter with people, tasks, and circumstances.
- **Continuity Force (⟶■)**: The force of persistence — the pull to maintain existing form, pattern, obligation, or identity.

The Qualitative Force has some resonance with epistemic drive (both concern the pull toward novelty and aliveness). The Contact Force has resonance with the pragmatic component (both concern actual consequence and real-world encounter). The Continuity Force has resonance with prior precision and model inertia (both describe the tendency of established patterns to persist).

These mappings are suggestive rather than precise. The Forces are phenomenologically described pulls within a lived system; expected free energy components are mathematical quantities in a probabilistic model. The conceptual territory overlaps but the formalisms are different.

### Layer 2 Recursive System Scales ↔ Nested Markov blankets

**Strength: Good structural analogy**

Active Inference's nested Markov blankets describe systems within systems: cells nested within organs, organs within organisms, organisms within social groups, groups within institutions. Each level has its own generative model, its own prediction error, its own active boundary. Patterns at one level constrain and shape patterns at adjacent levels.

TIF's Layer 2 Recursive System Scales describe agents, relations, and fields nested recursively — with mechanics recurring at each scale and patterns at one scale sustaining or blocking patterns at another.

Both frameworks independently arrive at the same structural conclusion: analysis must be scale-sensitive, and a pattern apparent at one scale may be sustained by processes at a different scale. This convergence is structurally significant.

## Mismatches

### Reductive vs. descriptive

Active Inference is reductive in a specific and intentional way: it claims that all behavior — perception, action, attention, learning, social dynamics — can be explained as prediction error minimization. It is a normative mathematical framework with explanatory ambition.

TIF is descriptive and diagnostic. It does not claim to reduce experience to a single driving principle. It provides a grammar for noticing, comparing, and navigating patterns — but it does not claim that all patterns reduce to one mechanism.

This is a genuine mismatch, not just a translation gap. Using TIF as if it were a predictive model would distort it. Using Active Inference as if its formal machinery captured the full richness of TIF's phenomenological language would distort that.

### Quality is not precision

Active Inference brackets phenomenal consciousness. It can describe when and how strongly something is attended to, but it does not (and does not claim to) explain what it is like for something to matter.

Quality (★) in TIF is precisely about felt character — the qualitative texture of experience, the sense that something is alive, interesting, beautiful, or wrong before interpretation catches up. Reducing this to precision loses the starting point of TIF's entire phenomenological project.

The translation "Quality ↔ precision" is useful for structural comparisons. It should not be treated as a reduction.

### Operations are not motor commands

TIF's Three Operations (Explore, Coordinate, Stabilize) are practical moves available to an agent, practitioner, or analyst. They are prescriptive in an intentional, agentic sense: things a person can choose to do.

Active Inference's action is technically "inference about motor commands" — a different logical structure. In Active Inference, action is not something the agent *chooses* in the ordinary sense; it is the result of minimizing expected free energy given prior beliefs about what a system like oneself would do.

These are not the same concept. Forcing them into equivalence distorts both frameworks.

### Misdirected (↯) has no clean equivalent

TIF's Misdirected distortion describes energy flowing in the wrong direction — effort, attention, or force that is real and active but not oriented toward what the system actually needs.

Active Inference's closest candidates — aberrant precision (in psychosis models) or goal misdirection (in reward model failure) — capture some aspects but not the phenomenological character of Misdirected. The TIF concept is more agnostic about mechanism; the AI concepts are tightly tied to specific formal structures.

### Relational scales are not naturally AI categories

Active Inference has been extended to social dynamics (collective active inference, group generative models), but these extensions treat social systems primarily as collections of agents modeling each other. The Relation Scale and Field Scale in TIF — particularly the implicit contract as a Relation–Field structure — are not natural categories in standard Active Inference.

TIF's relational scales describe *between* structures with their own Forces, Capacities, and Operations. Active Inference tends to locate agency in individual agents, with social dynamics emerging from their mutual modeling. This is a genuine structural difference.

## Cautions and non-claims

- TIF is not derived from Active Inference. The resonances are convergent, not genealogical.
- Active Inference does not validate TIF. Convergence between frameworks is suggestive, not proof.
- The most robust translations (Contact/prediction error; Continuity/generative model; recursive scales/nested Markov blankets) are structural analogies, not formal equivalences.
- Quality (★) should not be reduced to precision. The phenomenological starting point of TIF is not captured by any purely statistical quantity.
- Misdirected (↯) should not be forced into AI equivalence. The mapping breaks before it becomes useful.
- The relational scales (Relation Scale, Field Scale) are not natural AI categories. Extending Active Inference to social dynamics is active research territory with significant open questions.
- Using Active Inference formalism to add pseudo-precision to TIF claims would distort both frameworks. The Bridge is a comparison tool, not a derivation.

## Worked comparison: The cinema field

The cinema example (see `worked-example-cinema-as-field.md`) maps productively onto Active Inference at Field Scale.

**In TIF:** The cinema operates with an implicit community contract — an unspoken, un-negotiated set of expectations about what the cinema is and what it owes. This contract functions as the cinema field's Continuity structure. The field is structurally Tight: demand reliably exceeds capacity, and this is a field-level condition, not a personal failure. The repair requires renegotiating the implicit contract — making some expectations explicit and revising others.

**In Active Inference terms:** The implicit community contract is the cinema's field-level generative model — the prior belief structure about expected states (the cinema will be open, consistent, warm, communally responsive). The structural Tight condition is sustained high free energy: the cinema's actual capacity consistently falls short of what the generative model predicts as expected states, and this cannot be resolved through action alone because the generative model itself is generating unrealistic priors.

The repair TIF names — making the implicit contract explicit and renegotiating it — maps directly onto Active Inference's account of model revision: the problem is not reducible to better action within the current model; the generative model (the implicit contract) needs to be updated. Reducing free energy by revising priors rather than by effort alone.

**What the comparison adds:** The Active Inference framing makes it formally precise *why* individual efficiency improvements (better action) cannot resolve structural Tight: if the generative model continues generating expectations that exceed capacity, prediction error will persist regardless of how efficiently the system acts. The repair must include model revision (prior updating), not just better performance.

**What the TIF framing adds:** The Active Inference account is mechanism-focused and does not distinguish between the phenomenological character of different kinds of field pressure. TIF's distinction between Tight (demand exceeds capacity), Slack (under-engaged), and Misdirected (energy in wrong direction) is finer-grained for practical diagnosis, even without formalization.

**The convergence:** Both frameworks arrive at the same conclusion by different routes — the problem is structural, the repair requires changing the governing model, and individual-level intervention within an unchanged structural model will keep failing.
