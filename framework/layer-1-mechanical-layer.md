## 1. Purpose

Layer 1 is the mechanical layer of The Triadic Integrity Framework.

It translates the structural grammar of Layer 0 into a working model.

Layer 0 asks:

```text
What are the basic structural ingredients?
```

Layer 1 asks:

```text
How do those ingredients behave as a working system?
```

Layer 1 is called “mechanical” because it describes:

- forces
- capacities
- operations
- relations
- distortions
- formations

This does not mean the framework treats human experience as a literal machine.

It means the framework uses mechanical language to make patterns visible, traceable, and usable.

---

## 2. Core summary

Layer 1 contains three mechanical systems:

- **⟶ The Three Forces**
- **⬡ The Three Capacities**
- **↻ The Three Operations**

Preferred summary:

> **⟶ Forces generate movement. ⬡ Capacities determine what the system can bear. ↻ Operations tune the system toward responsive integrity.**

At a practical level, Layer 1 asks:

```text
What is trying to happen?          → Forces
What can the system actually hold? → Capacities
What move is available?            → Operations
```

---

## 3. Relationship to Layer 0

Layer 0 gives the structural primitives:

- **★ Quality**
- **● Contact**
- **■ Continuity**

Layer 1 translates those primitives into mechanical behavior.

| Layer 0 primitive | Layer 1 mechanical expression |
|---|---|
| **★ Quality** | qualitative force, salience, felt charge, intensity, regulatory capacity, exploration |
| **● Contact** | contact force, encounter, resistance, relation, structural capacity, coordination |
| **■ Continuity** | continuity force, pattern, persistence, form, integrity capacity, stabilization |

Layer 1 should always remain traceable back to Layer 0.

---

## 4. Component grammar

Each Layer 1 system contains seven components:

```text
3 elements + 3 relations + 1 emergent formation
```

The component types are:

| Symbol | Component type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **○** | **Element** | A basic point, force, capacity, or operation inside a system. |
| **⟷** | **Relation** | A two-part relation between elements. |
| **△** | **Emergent Formation** | The formed whole that emerges when the elements and relations cohere. |

Compact grammar:

```text
○ element → ⟷ relation → △ emergent formation
```

The elements are not isolated units.

The relations are not optional extras.

The emergent formation is not a fourth element. It is the formed whole generated by the relations among the components.

# System 1: ⟶ The Three Forces

## 7. Overview

**⟶ The Three Forces** describe what generates movement.

They are the system’s active directional tendencies.

They answer:

```text
What is trying to happen?
```

The three Force elements are:

| ID | Component | Layer 0 root | Short definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **⟶○★** | **Qualitative Force** | **★ Quality** | The force of felt salience, vividness, intensity, or qualitative charge. |
| **⟶○●** | **Contact Force** | **● Contact** | The force of encounter, resistance, friction, relation, or actuality. |
| **⟶○■** | **Continuity Force** | **■ Continuity** | The force of pattern, persistence, repetition, stabilization, or continuing form. |

---

## 8. ⟶○★ Qualitative Force

**⟶○★ Qualitative Force** is the force of felt salience.

It describes the way something becomes vivid, charged, interesting, attractive, repellent, beautiful, strange, meaningful, or otherwise attention-shaping.

Qualitative Force is not identical to desire. Desire may arise from it, but Qualitative Force is more basic. It is the force exerted by the felt character of experience.

Examples:

- an idea having charge
- a color or image drawing attention
- a phrase feeling exactly right or wrong
- a possibility becoming vivid
- a situation having a strong felt tone

---

## 9. ⟶○● Contact Force

**⟶○● Contact Force** is the force of encounter.

It describes the way reality, otherness, resistance, feedback, friction, or relation presses on the system.

Contact Force is not merely social relation or interpersonal harmony. It includes any encounter with what is actual and not fully under one’s control.

Examples:

- a plan meeting real constraints
- another person responding unexpectedly
- a body encountering fatigue
- an idea being tested in practice
- an environment pushing back

---

## 10. ⟶○■ Continuity Force

**⟶○■ Continuity Force** is the force of continuing form.

It describes the way patterns, habits, structures, memories, routines, standards, or forms exert force over time.

Continuity Force is not the same as rigidity. It can be adaptive, living, and flexible. It names the force of persistence and recognizable form.

Examples:

- a habit repeating
- a routine pulling the day into shape
- a role carrying expectations
- a story retaining its pattern
- a structure continuing to organize behaviour

---

## 11. ⟶ Force relations

The three Forces generate three relations.

| ID | Component | Relation | Short definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **⟶⟷★●** | **Quality–Contact Force Relation** | **⟶○★ + ⟶○●** | Qualitative Force meeting Contact Force. |
| **⟶⟷●■** | **Contact–Continuity Force Relation** | **⟶○● + ⟶○■** | Contact Force becoming patterned over time. |
| **⟶⟷★■** | **Quality–Continuity Force Relation** | **⟶○★ + ⟶○■** | Qualitative Force persisting as recognizable form. |

---

## 12. ⟶⟷★● Quality–Contact Force Relation

**⟶⟷★● Quality–Contact Force Relation** is the relation between felt salience and encounter.

It describes what happens when a vivid or charged quality meets reality, resistance, feedback, specificity, or interaction.

Without Contact Force, Qualitative Force can remain private, atmospheric, untested, or impulsive.

Without Qualitative Force, Contact Force can become bare collision, friction, or mechanical interaction.

Together, they create vivid encounter.

Examples:

- an exciting idea being tested in conversation
- a feeling becoming sharper when expressed
- a beautiful possibility meeting practical constraint
- a draft being shown to another person
- a vague impression becoming specific through contact

---

## 13. ⟶⟷●■ Contact–Continuity Force Relation

**⟶⟷●■ Contact–Continuity Force Relation** is the relation between encounter and continuing pattern.

It describes what happens when contact with reality becomes repeatable, reliable, consequential, or structurally patterned.

Without Continuity Force, Contact Force can remain episodic, reactive, or disconnected.

Without Contact Force, Continuity Force can become abstract, stale, or detached from reality.

Together, they create reliable relation.

Examples:

- repeated practice forming skill
- recurring feedback revealing a pattern
- a conversation becoming a relationship
- a one-time adjustment becoming a routine
- friction turning into a stable constraint

---

## 14. ⟶⟷★■ Quality–Continuity Force Relation

**⟶⟷★■ Quality–Continuity Force Relation** is the relation between felt salience and continuing form.

It describes what happens when something vivid, charged, beautiful, meaningful, or interesting persists across time and becomes recognizable.

Without Continuity Force, Qualitative Force can flare and disappear.

Without Qualitative Force, Continuity Force can persist without felt significance.

Together, they create recognizable salience.

Examples:

- a style becoming identifiable
- an idea retaining its importance over time
- a melody remaining recognizable across variations
- a value continuing to matter
- an image or phrase retaining its charge

---

## 15. ⟶△ Force Formation

**⟶△ Force Formation** is the emergent formation of **⟶ The Three Forces**.

It forms when:

- **⟶○★ Qualitative Force** gives felt salience
- **⟶○● Contact Force** brings encounter with reality
- **⟶○■ Continuity Force** provides pattern and persistence
- the three Force relations become mutually informing

**⟶△ Force Formation** is not a fourth Force.

It is the formed whole that emerges when the Forces are coherent enough to generate movement with integrity.

A formed Force system has:

- enough qualitative charge to matter
- enough contact to stay real
- enough continuity to hold form over time

In the Three Pulls Register, this may be translated as:

```text
Wholesome Triangle
```

# System 2: ⬡ The Three Capacities

## 16. Overview

**⬡ The Three Capacities** describe what the system can bear.

They are the supports, bounds, containers, and load-bearing conditions that allow Forces to function without collapse, overstrain, or distortion.

They answer:

```text
What can this system actually hold?
```

The three Capacity elements are:

| ID | Component | Layer 0 root | Short definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **⬡○★** | **Bodily/Regulatory Capacity** | **★ Quality** | The capacity to regulate intensity, sensation, affect, energy, and bodily state. |
| **⬡○●** | **Social/Structural Capacity** | **● Contact** | The capacity provided by relational, social, environmental, and structural support. |
| **⬡○■** | **Form/Integrity Capacity** | **■ Continuity** | The capacity of a form to hold together, remain legible, and preserve integrity. |

---

## 17. ⬡○★ Bodily/Regulatory Capacity

**⬡○★ Bodily/Regulatory Capacity** is the system’s ability to bear intensity from within.

It includes regulation, sensation, affective bandwidth, nervous system capacity, energy, fatigue, stress tolerance, and embodied readiness.

Examples:

- having enough energy to engage
- being able to stay with strong feeling
- calming down after activation
- noticing bodily signals
- having enough rest, food, movement, or recovery

---

## 18. ⬡○● Social/Structural Capacity

**⬡○● Social/Structural Capacity** is the system’s support from relation and environment.

It includes social support, role clarity, shared expectations, tools, infrastructure, time, money, agreements, and external structure.

Examples:

- having help from another person
- having clear expectations
- having enough time or space
- having a stable workplace structure
- having tools that support the task

---

## 19. ⬡○■ Form/Integrity Capacity

**⬡○■ Form/Integrity Capacity** is the system’s ability to hold coherent shape.

It includes boundaries, form, structure, legibility, craft, standards, internal coherence, and the ability to remain recognizable without becoming rigid.

Examples:

- a clear outline
- a stable practice
- a coherent argument
- a reliable container
- a form that can flex without falling apart

---

## 20. ⬡ Capacity relations

The three Capacities generate three relations.

| ID | Component | Relation | Short definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **⬡⟷★●** | **Bodily/Regulatory–Social/Structural Capacity Relation** | **⬡○★ + ⬡○●** | Bodily regulation supported by social or structural conditions. |
| **⬡⟷●■** | **Social/Structural–Form/Integrity Capacity Relation** | **⬡○● + ⬡○■** | External support organized into coherent form. |
| **⬡⟷★■** | **Bodily/Regulatory–Form/Integrity Capacity Relation** | **⬡○★ + ⬡○■** | Inner regulation aligned with coherent form. |

---

## 21. ⬡⟷★● Bodily/Regulatory–Social/Structural Capacity Relation

**⬡⟷★● Bodily/Regulatory–Social/Structural Capacity Relation** is the relation between bodily regulation and social or structural support.

It describes how a person or system becomes more able to bear intensity when the surrounding conditions help regulate the load.

Examples:

- a calm person helping another person settle
- a clear schedule reducing stress
- a supportive room making a hard conversation possible
- a tool or checklist lowering cognitive burden

Human-register phrase:

```text
Co-Regulated Support
```

---

## 22. ⬡⟷●■ Social/Structural–Form/Integrity Capacity Relation

**⬡⟷●■ Social/Structural–Form/Integrity Capacity Relation** is the relation between external support and coherent form.

It describes how social, environmental, or structural conditions become organized into something understandable and usable.

Examples:

- a role becoming clear
- a project plan making work easier to coordinate
- a shared agreement creating stability
- a workflow making responsibilities legible

Human-register phrase:

```text
Legible Structure
```

---

## 23. ⬡⟷★■ Bodily/Regulatory–Form/Integrity Capacity Relation

**⬡⟷★■ Bodily/Regulatory–Form/Integrity Capacity Relation** is the relation between internal regulation and coherent form.

It describes how a form can be held in a way that remains embodied, regulated, and alive rather than brittle or dissociated.

Examples:

- maintaining a boundary without panic
- holding a posture of calm authority
- sustaining a practice without overstraining
- keeping standards without becoming rigid

Human-register phrase:

```text
Embodied Integrity
```

---

## 24. ⬡△ Capacity Formation

**⬡△ Capacity Formation** is the emergent formation of **⬡ The Three Capacities**.

It forms when:

- **⬡○★ Bodily/Regulatory Capacity** can bear intensity
- **⬡○● Social/Structural Capacity** provides support and contact
- **⬡○■ Form/Integrity Capacity** holds coherent shape
- the three Capacity relations become mutually supportive

**⬡△ Capacity Formation** is not a fourth Capacity.

It is the formed whole that emerges when the system can bear what is moving through it.

A formed Capacity system has:

- enough regulation to stay with intensity
- enough support to stay in contact
- enough form to remain coherent

Human-register phrase:

```text
Held Capacity
```

# System 3: ↻ The Three Operations

## 25. Overview

**↻ The Three Operations** describe what the system can do to tune itself.

They are adaptive moves.

They help bring Forces and Capacities into better relation.

They answer:

```text
What move is available?
```

The three Operation elements are:

| ID | Component | Layer 0 root | Short definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **↻○★** | **Explore Operation** | **★ Quality** | Follow, test, or increase salience and possibility. |
| **↻○●** | **Coordinate Operation** | **● Contact** | Bring the system into better contact, relation, feedback, or alignment. |
| **↻○■** | **Stabilize Operation** | **■ Continuity** | Create, maintain, or restore continuity and form. |

---

## 26. ↻○★ Explore Operation

**↻○★ Explore Operation** is the move toward salience, possibility, and discovery.

It asks what is alive, interesting, vivid, unresolved, promising, or worth investigating.

Examples:

- trying a first draft
- following curiosity
- opening a question
- testing a possibility
- noticing what has energy

---

## 27. ↻○● Coordinate Operation

**↻○● Coordinate Operation** is the move toward contact, relation, feedback, and alignment.

It asks what needs to be brought into contact with reality, another person, a constraint, or a shared situation.

Examples:

- asking for clarification
- checking the actual conditions
- syncing with another person
- getting feedback
- adjusting to what is really happening

---

## 28. ↻○■ Stabilize Operation

**↻○■ Stabilize Operation** is the move toward continuity, form, and repeatability.

It asks what needs to be held, simplified, structured, repeated, or made durable.

Examples:

- writing down the decision
- creating a routine
- setting a boundary
- making a template
- preserving what worked

---

## 29. ↻ Operation relations

The three Operations generate three relations.

| ID | Component | Relation | Short definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **↻⟷★●** | **Explore–Coordinate Operation Relation** | **↻○★ + ↻○●** | Explore by bringing possibility into contact with reality. |
| **↻⟷●■** | **Coordinate–Stabilize Operation Relation** | **↻○● + ↻○■** | Coordinate in a way that stabilizes into reliable form. |
| **↻⟷★■** | **Explore–Stabilize Operation Relation** | **↻○★ + ↻○■** | Explore within enough structure to learn and retain value. |

---

## 30. ↻⟷★● Explore–Coordinate Operation Relation

**↻⟷★● Explore–Coordinate Operation Relation** is the relation between exploration and coordination.

It describes the move of bringing a possibility into real encounter.

Examples:

- showing a rough draft to someone
- testing an idea in practice
- asking whether a plan works for another person
- trying the thing instead of only imagining it

Human-register phrase:

```text
Test in Contact
```

---

## 31. ↻⟷●■ Coordinate–Stabilize Operation Relation

**↻⟷●■ Coordinate–Stabilize Operation Relation** is the relation between coordination and stabilization.

It describes the move of turning contact, feedback, or negotiation into a stable and usable form.

Examples:

- turning a conversation into an agreement
- turning feedback into a revised plan
- turning a conflict into a clearer boundary
- turning a recurring issue into a routine or system

Human-register phrase:

```text
Integrated Alignment
```

---

## 32. ↻⟷★■ Explore–Stabilize Operation Relation

**↻⟷★■ Explore–Stabilize Operation Relation** is the relation between exploration and stabilization.

It describes the move of preserving enough structure for exploration to become learnable, repeatable, and useful.

Examples:

- keeping notes while experimenting
- creating a small container for creative work
- trying variations while preserving the core form
- giving curiosity a repeatable practice

Human-register phrase:

```text
Disciplined Experimentation
```

---

## 33. ↻△ Operation Formation

**↻△ Operation Formation** is the emergent formation of **↻ The Three Operations**.

It forms when:

- **↻○★ Explore Operation** opens possibility
- **↻○● Coordinate Operation** brings contact and alignment
- **↻○■ Stabilize Operation** creates continuity
- the three Operation relations become mutually supportive

**↻△ Operation Formation** is not a fourth Operation.

It is the formed whole that emerges when the system can keep tuning itself.

A formed Operation system has:

- enough exploration to stay alive
- enough coordination to stay in contact
- enough stabilization to retain what works

Human-register phrase:

```text
Adaptive Practice
```

# Diagnostic Overlay

## 34. Overview

Layer 1 is the main home of the diagnostic overlay.

The three diagnostic distortions are:

- **∿ Slack**
- **≡ Tight**
- **↯ Misdirected**

The canonical well-formed condition is:

- **✓ Coherent Formation**

Important distinction:

```text
∿ Slack, ≡ Tight, and ↯ Misdirected are distortion patterns.
✓ Coherent Formation is the well-formed condition.
```

**✓ Coherent Formation** is not a fourth distortion.

It is the condition in which a component, relation, or system is formed coherently enough to function with responsive integrity.

---

## 35. ∿ Slack

**∿ Slack** describes insufficient activation, support, force, relation, structure, or form.

A Slack pattern may appear as:

- underpowered
- vague
- unsupported
- diffuse
- avoidant
- weakly held
- not enough force
- not enough structure
- not enough contact

Examples:

| Component | Slack pattern |
|---|---|
| **⟶○★ Qualitative Force** | Not enough felt energy, vividness, salience, or qualitative charge. |
| **⬡○● Social/Structural Capacity** | Not enough support, structure, agreement, or external scaffolding. |
| **↻○■ Stabilize Operation** | Not enough follow-through, continuity, routine, or form. |

---

## 36. ≡ Tight

**≡ Tight** describes excessive activation, pressure, rigidity, force, constraint, or over-control.

A Tight pattern may appear as:

- pressured
- over-controlled
- rigid
- brittle
- compulsive
- clenched
- over-structured
- unable to flex
- too much force
- too much pressure

Examples:

| Component | Tight pattern |
|---|---|
| **⟶○■ Continuity Force** | Too much insistence on control, sameness, predictability, or fixed form. |
| **⬡○★ Bodily/Regulatory Capacity** | The body is over-braced, over-controlled, or unable to relax. |
| **↻○● Coordinate Operation** | Too much managing, checking, negotiating, or forcing alignment. |

---

## 37. ↯ Misdirected

**↯ Misdirected** describes skewed, misaligned, misplaced, confused, or wrongly aimed force, support, relation, or operation.

A Misdirected pattern may appear as:

- aimed at the wrong thing
- based on a misread
- out of alignment
- solving the wrong problem
- misplaced intensity
- false stability
- distorted relation
- wrong geometry

Examples:

| Component | Misdirected pattern |
|---|---|
| **⟶○★ Qualitative Force** | Energy or interest is real, but aimed at the wrong object. |
| **⬡○■ Form/Integrity Capacity** | The form is coherent in itself but wrong for the situation. |
| **↻○★ Explore Operation** | Exploration becomes novelty-chasing or avoidance of the real issue. |

---

## 38. ✓ Coherent Formation

**✓ Coherent Formation** is the well-formed condition of a component, relation, or system.

It means the relevant parts are functioning together with enough responsive integrity.

A coherently formed system is not perfect, static, or permanently healthy.

It is formed enough to work.

A pattern of **✓ Coherent Formation** may appear as:

- appropriately energized
- sufficiently supported
- responsive to contact
- stable without rigidity
- alive without chaos
- structured without over-control
- adaptive without losing form
- clear enough for a next move

Examples:

| Component | Coherent Formation pattern |
|---|---|
| **⟶△ Force Formation** | The Forces are charged, contacted, and continuous enough to generate movement with integrity. |
| **⬡△ Capacity Formation** | The system can bear the forces moving through it. |
| **↻△ Operation Formation** | The system can explore, coordinate, and stabilize in a continuing cycle of adjustment. |

---

## 39. Applying Layer 1

When applying Layer 1 to a situation, use this sequence:

```text
1. What is trying to happen?          → Forces
2. What can the system actually hold? → Capacities
3. What move is available?            → Operations
4. Is the pattern Slack, Tight, or Misdirected?
5. What would Coherent Formation require?
```

Layer 1 should help locate the main pattern of stuckness.

It should not create unnecessary complexity.

If a practical answer becomes overloaded with symbols, return to plain language.

## 44. Working rule

When developing or applying Layer 1, ask:
