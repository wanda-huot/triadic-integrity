# Layer 2: Recursive System Scales

**Status:** Working draft — v2.0
**Author:** Gwendolyn Huot

---

## Purpose

Layer 2 situates the framework's structural and mechanical grammar within recursive scales of organization. Where Layer 0 names the primitives and Layer 1 describes how they move and malfunction, Layer 2 asks: *at what scale is this happening?*

The three primitives — Quality, Contact, Continuity — operate at every scale of organized experience. The goal of Layer 2 is not to add new primitives, but to identify the scale at which a given pattern is operating, and to notice how patterns at different scales interact.

---

## The Seven Scales

Layer 2 organizes experience across seven scales. The structure mirrors the broader triadic grammar: three elemental scales, three relational scales, and one emergent whole.

---

### ○ 1. Agent Scale

**The individual organism, person, or agent.**

The smallest coherent unit of analysis: a body, a self, a decision-maker, an experiencing subject. At this scale, the question is what is happening *within* the agent — their forces, capacities, and operations as a bounded system.

Typical questions at this scale:
- What is this person trying to do or sustain?
- What can they actually bear right now?
- What moves are available to them?

---

### ○ 2. Relation Scale

**The dyad, encounter, exchange, or relationship.**

Two or more agents in contact. At this scale, the unit of analysis is the *between* — the quality and dynamics of the encounter itself, not just what each party brings to it.

Typical questions at this scale:
- What is this relationship or encounter trying to produce?
- What is the relational field capable of holding?
- What adjustment is available within the exchange?

---

### ○ 3. Field Scale

**The wider context, group, culture, ecology, or environment.**

The distributed surround within which agents and relations are embedded. At this scale, patterns are held collectively — in norms, institutions, physical environments, shared symbolic worlds, ecological systems.

Typical questions at this scale:
- What patterns does this context sustain or suppress?
- What does this field reward, punish, or make invisible?
- What structural affordances or constraints are in play?

---

### ⟷ 4. Agent–Relation Scale

**How individuals form dyads, attachments, roles, and conversations.**

The interface between the individual and their relational world. This scale attends to how an agent enters, maintains, or exits relational structures — how they attach, communicate, negotiate, and are shaped by encounters with others.

Typical questions at this scale:
- How is this person showing up in their relationships?
- What relational patterns does this individual tend to generate or attract?
- Where is there mismatch between what the agent brings and what the relation requires?

---

### ⟷ 5. Relation–Field Scale

**How relationships become norms, groups, institutions, and shared patterns.**

The interface between relational dynamics and wider structural patterns. Repeated relational exchanges crystallize into roles, norms, organizations, and institutions. This scale attends to how the interpersonal becomes structural.

Typical questions at this scale:
- What relational patterns are being reproduced at scale?
- How are group norms, institutional rules, or cultural expectations shaping this relationship?
- What would it take to change the structural pattern, not just the individual exchange?

---

### ⟷ 6. Agent–Field Scale

**How individuals are shaped by culture, place, infrastructure, language, and symbolic worlds.**

The interface between the individual and the wider field. Agents do not exist in isolation — they are shaped by history, language, embodied environment, economic structure, and inherited symbolic systems. This scale attends to those mutual influences.

Typical questions at this scale:
- What field conditions have shaped this agent's capacities, distortions, and available moves?
- How is the agent, in turn, contributing to or resisting field-level patterns?
- What is invisible to the agent because it is structural?

---

### △ 7. Recursive System Ecology

**The emergent whole: agents, relations, and fields recursively shaping one another across scales.**

No scale is self-contained. Agents shape relations; relations shape fields; fields shape agents. This scale names the whole recursive dynamic — the ecology of mutual causation across all levels.

At this scale, the framework is less a diagnostic tool and more an orientation: *where is the pattern being held, and at what scale does it need to be addressed?*

Typical questions at this scale:
- At what scale is this problem most tractable?
- Is this being addressed at the right level, or is it being addressed at the wrong scale (e.g., treating a field-level problem as an agent-level failure)?
- What would a coherent formation look like across all three scales simultaneously?

## Structural Summary

| Icon | Scale | Unit of Analysis |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| ○ | Agent | Individual organism, person, or agent |
| ○ | Relation | Dyad, encounter, exchange, or relationship |
| ○ | Field | Group, culture, ecology, environment |
| ⟷ | Agent–Relation | How individuals form and inhabit relational structures |
| ⟷ | Relation–Field | How relationships crystallize into norms and institutions |
| ⟷ | Agent–Field | How individuals and cultural structures shape each other |
| △ | Recursive System Ecology | The emergent whole — mutual causation across all scales |

This mirrors the Layer 0 grammar: three primitives, three dyads, one emergent whole.

## Layer 1 at every scale

At every Layer 2 scale, Layer 1 asks the same three mechanical questions:

```text
Forces:     What is trying to happen at this scale?
Capacities: What can this scale actually hold?
Operations: What move can transform or tune the pattern at this scale?
```

The Layer 1 diagnostic overlay (Slack, Tight, Misdirected, Coherent Formation) applies at every scale. A pattern that is Tight at Agent Scale may be Slack at Relation–Field Scale. Scale-aware diagnosis requires checking each relevant scale independently.

---

## Presentation scale, sustaining scale, repair scale

A core v2 diagnostic rule:

> A problem may have a **presentation scale**, a **sustaining scale**, and a **repair scale**. These are often not the same.

- **Presentation scale:** The scale where the problem is felt or noticed.
- **Sustaining scale:** The scale where the pattern is maintained and reproduced.
- **Repair scale:** The scale where an intervention would actually change the pattern.

Addressing only the presentation scale often fails because the sustaining structure remains intact. v2 makes this error visible and nameable.

---

## Relationship to Layers 0 and 1

Layer 2 does not replace Layers 0 and 1. It locates them.

- **Layer 0** names what is structurally present at any scale (Quality, Contact, Continuity).
- **Layer 1** describes how the system is moving and where it is malfunctioning (Forces, Capacities, Operations; Slack, Tight, Misdirected).
- **Layer 2** identifies *which scale* is in question, and whether the diagnostic or repair work needs to happen at a different scale than it is currently being attempted.

A common diagnostic error is **scale mismatch**: treating an Agent–Field problem as if it were only an Agent problem, or trying to repair a Relation–Field pattern through individual-level intervention alone.

---

## Relationship to the Applied Framework

Layer 2 is a structural layer, not an applied one. It does not specify how to speak about these scales in any particular human domain — that work belongs to the Registers (Applied Framework).

The Registers translate these scales into human-facing vocabulary for specific contexts: relational, organizational, therapeutic, parenting, and so on.

---

## Design Note

Layer 2 is recursive by design. The triadic grammar (three elements, three relations, one emergent whole) appears at every scale — and Layer 2 itself is organized by that same grammar. This recursion is a feature, not a coincidence.
