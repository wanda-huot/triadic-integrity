# Worked Example: Framecraft vs. Screening Room

**Status:** Working example — v2.0  
**Category:** Applied Framework / Worked Example  
**Framework:** The Triadic Integrity Framework  
**Author:** Gwendolyn Huot with Wanda

---

## The situation

Gwendolyn is pulled toward Framecraft work while Screening Room work also needs attention.

This is a recurring pattern: intellectual and creative energy pulls toward the framework project; operational and responsibility pulls toward the cinema. Both are real. Neither is wrong. But together they create friction.

---

## Why this example matters for v2

This is the kind of case where v1 and v2 give meaningfully different answers.

**v1 would diagnose this as an Agent-Scale problem:**
- Competing forces in one person
- A capacity issue (she can't do everything)
- A diagnostic: probably Tight or Misdirected
- A repair: regulate better, prioritize, find a better next move

That analysis is not wrong. But it is incomplete.

**v2 asks a prior question: at what scale is this actually happening?**

The answer turns out to be multi-scale. The problem exists simultaneously at Agent Scale, Agent–Field Scale, and Relation–Field Scale — and the v1 diagnosis misses the structural dimension entirely.

---

## The core v2 diagnostic rule

Before the scale-by-scale analysis, name the key v2 concept this example depends on.

**At every scale, Layer 1 asks the same three mechanical questions:**

```text
Forces:     What is trying to happen at this scale?
Capacities: What can this scale actually hold?
Operations: What move can transform or tune the pattern at this scale?
```

**And v2 adds a diagnostic rule about scale:**

> A problem may have a **presentation scale**, a **sustaining scale**, and a **repair scale**. These are often not the same.

For this example:

| Scale type | Scale | What it names |
|---|---|---|
| **Presentation scale** | Agent Scale | Gwendolyn feels pulled, pressured, and guilty. The tension is felt here. |
| **Sustaining scale** | Agent–Field Scale | Cinema and Framecraft have unequal legitimacy, structure, and external accountability. The tension is maintained here. |
| **Repair scale** | Relation–Field Scale | Create an enacted boundary between the two fields. The fix needs to happen here. |

This is the core v2 hinge. Addressing only the presentation scale — regulating at Agent Scale — will not resolve a problem sustained at a structural level. v1 could name the symptom. v2 can locate where the repair needs to happen.

---

## Layer 2 analysis

### ○ Agent Scale

At Agent Scale, the pattern looks like competing forces within Gwendolyn.

- **Qualitative Force (⟶★):** Intellectual aliveness, curiosity, the pull toward Framecraft. This force is vivid, charged, and strongly felt.
- **Continuity Force (⟶■):** The ongoing form of her responsibility — cinema owner, boss, community steward. This force is stable, persistent, and socially embedded.
- **Contact Force (⟶●):** The actual demands of each day — what is actually in front of her, what is asking for attention right now.

**Diagnostic at Agent Scale: ≡ Tight / ↯ Misdirected**

The forces are real and both valid. The problem at this scale is not that either force is wrong — it's that they have no container. Both are pulling without structure. The system is too tight (pressure without a clear outlet) and occasionally misdirected (Framecraft energy bleeding into cinema hours, or cinema guilt bleeding into creative time).

**What coherent formation looks like at Agent Scale:**
A person who can hold both forces without one collapsing the other. The quality of intellectual aliveness can coexist with the continuity of operational responsibility — if the agent has a container for each.

But this coherent formation is **not achievable through individual willpower alone.** That's the key v2 insight. The problem at Agent Scale has structural causes that need to be addressed at a different scale.

---

### ⟷ Agent–Field Scale

At Agent–Field Scale, the analysis shifts: Gwendolyn is not just a person with competing feelings. She is a person embedded in two different fields, and each field makes different demands on her.

**Field 1: The Screening Room**
- This is an established, socially real field. It has staff, customers, community relationships, deadlines, financial stakes, and a clear public identity.
- The field is strong and defined. It rewards cinema-related activity and makes Gwendolyn's role as owner legible.
- Its implicit rule: *there is always more you could do for the cinema.*

**Field 2: Framecraft / Sensemaking Community**
- This field is aspirational and partially inhabited. It exists — Yibbam, Wanda, the GitHub repository, intellectual community — but it does not yet have the same social density or external accountability structure as the cinema.
- Its implicit rule: *this is optional, it can wait, it needs to earn its place.*

**Diagnostic at Agent–Field Scale: ↯ Misdirected**

The misdirection here is structural, not personal. The cinema field is over-legible (its demands are always visible and justified). The Framecraft field is under-legible (its demands are invisible or feel self-indulgent). This asymmetry means Gwendolyn's attention is systematically pulled toward the cinema even when cinema work doesn't genuinely need her and Framecraft work does.

This is not a willpower failure. It is a **field-structure problem**: one field has strong institutional affordances (staff, customers, rent) and the other does not yet.

**What coherent formation looks like at Agent–Field Scale:**
The Framecraft field gains enough structure and social reality that it can make legitimate claims on Gwendolyn's time without having to justify itself against cinema logic every time. This might mean: a regular time boundary, a named practice, a community with real accountability, or simply the internal recognition that Framecraft is not optional — it is part of who she is and what she is building.

---

### ⟷ Relation–Field Scale

This scale asks: what structural norms govern when the cinema field ends and the Framecraft field begins?

Currently: none that are externally enforced. The boundary is entirely internal to Gwendolyn and therefore subject to the field-asymmetry described above.

**Diagnostic at Relation–Field Scale: ∿ Slack**

The interface between the two fields is under-formed. There is no agreed-upon schedule, norm, or structure that protects creative time from operational encroachment. Without that structure, the stronger field (cinema) will reliably colonize the weaker one (Framecraft).

**What coherent formation looks like at Relation–Field Scale:**
Some kind of enacted boundary — a schedule, a named ritual, a social commitment to the Framecraft practice — that functions as a structural separator between the two fields. This boundary doesn't need to be rigid; it just needs to exist and be honored.

---

### △ Recursive System Ecology

Zooming out: this is not a problem that will be solved by self-regulation alone. The three scales interact:

1. The **Agent–Field asymmetry** (cinema field is over-legible, Framecraft is under-legible) feeds the **Agent-Scale pressure** (competing forces with no container).
2. The **lack of structural boundary** (Relation–Field Slack) means the Agent–Field asymmetry has nothing to push against.
3. The Agent-Scale tension (feeling pulled, guilty, pressured) then reinforces the narrative that Framecraft is the optional/indulgent option.

This is a recursive loop, not a one-time decision problem.

**The repair needs to happen at multiple scales simultaneously:**

- *Agent Scale*: Acknowledge both forces as real and legitimate. Neither is wrong.
- *Agent–Field Scale*: Give the Framecraft field enough structure and social reality to become a legitimate competing claim on time.
- *Relation–Field Scale*: Create an enacted boundary between the two fields — a named practice, a time structure, something that has external reality.

---

## What this example demonstrates about v2

**What v1 could see:**
- Competing forces in one person
- A capacity constraint
- A repair direction: find a better next move, regulate better

**What v2 adds:**
- The diagnosis at Agent Scale is real but insufficient
- The root cause is structural: field asymmetry at Agent–Field Scale
- The repair requires work at a different scale than the symptom presents
- Individual regulation attempts will keep failing until the field-level structure changes

**The key v2 concepts this demonstrates:**

**Scale mismatch** — the problem presents at Agent Scale but requires repair at Agent–Field and Relation–Field scales. Trying to fix a field-structure problem through individual willpower is a common and frustrating error. v2 makes this error visible and nameable.

**Presentation / sustaining / repair scale distinction** — the scale where a problem is felt (presentation), the scale where it is maintained (sustaining), and the scale where the fix needs to land (repair) are often different. Identifying all three is the v2 diagnostic move.

---

## Practical moves

1. **Name the two fields explicitly.** Framecraft is not a hobby. It is a serious bounded creative practice. Naming it as such begins to give it field-level legitimacy.

2. **Create a boundary structure.** Even a simple one: Framecraft has a named time. That time is not available to the cinema unless genuinely urgent. The boundary doesn't need to be large — it needs to exist and be consistent.

3. **Find at least one external relationship that holds the Framecraft field.** A community of inquiry, a collaborator, a regular touchpoint. External accountability gives the field social density it currently lacks.

4. **Stop treating cinema attention as the default and Framecraft attention as the justifiable exception.** That framing is the field asymmetry speaking. Both are legitimate. Both deserve structure.
