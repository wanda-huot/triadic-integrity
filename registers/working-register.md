# The Working Register

**Status:** Working draft  
**Category:** Applied Framework / Human Register  
**Source layer:** Layer 1: The Mechanical Layer  
**Framework:** The Triadic Integrity Framework  
**Author:** Gwendolyn Huot with ChatGPT  

---

## 1. Purpose

**The Working Register** translates The Triadic Integrity Framework into the language of work, projects, effort, prioritization, burnout, execution, and practical decision-making.

It is for situations where the central question is:

```text
What needs doing, what can I actually handle, and what move would help?
```

The Working Register is not a productivity system by itself.

It is a register for interpreting work situations through the framework.

It should help clarify work, not moralize output.

---

## 2. Best used for

The Working Register is best used for:

- daily planning
- project planning
- prioritization
- burnout reflection
- creative blocks
- procrastination
- overwork
- leadership
- stuck projects
- deciding what matters now
- choosing the next useful action

It is especially useful when a person has too many possible tasks, unclear priorities, low capacity, or a mismatch between what matters and what they are actually doing.

## 4. Demands

**Demands** are the Working Register translation of **The Three Forces**.

They name what is generating movement, pressure, importance, or direction in a work situation.

The three Demands are:

| Working term | Canonical Layer 1 source | Short meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Stakes** | **Qualitative Force** | What matters, has value, or carries felt importance. |
| **Asks** | **Contact Force** | What reality, people, deadlines, systems, or circumstances are asking for. |
| **Standards** | **Continuity Force** | What level of quality, consistency, reliability, or form needs to be maintained. |

---

## 5. Stakes

**Stakes** are the work-facing translation of **Qualitative Force**.

Stakes name what matters in the work.

They may include:

- importance
- opportunity
- risk
- meaning
- audience value
- creative value
- emotional weight
- reputational weight
- financial consequence

Stakes ask:

```text
Why does this matter?
What has value here?
What is actually important?
What would be lost if this were ignored?
```

Stakes are not the same as urgency.

Something can matter deeply without being urgent.

Something can feel urgent without having high stakes.

---

## 6. Asks

**Asks** are the work-facing translation of **Contact Force**.

Asks name what the actual situation is requesting or requiring.

They may come from:

- customers
- coworkers
- collaborators
- deadlines
- emails
- children
- money
- logistics
- technical systems
- physical constraints
- the real state of a project

Asks ask:

```text
What is being asked of me?
Who or what needs a response?
What is reality requiring?
What contact point needs attention?
```

Asks are not always obligations.

Some asks can be declined, delayed, delegated, reduced, or reframed.

---

## 7. Standards

**Standards** are the work-facing translation of **Continuity Force**.

Standards name the level of quality, consistency, form, or reliability that the work needs to maintain.

They may include:

- accuracy
- polish
- consistency
- craft
- timing
- reliability
- house style
- professional expectation
- repeatable process
- “good enough” definitions

Standards ask:

```text
What level of quality is required?
What form does this need to hold?
What counts as good enough?
What should be preserved over time?
```

Standards are not always high.

Sometimes the right standard is intentionally modest.

---

## 8. Demand relations

The three Demands combine into three relations.

| Working term | Canonical Layer 1 source | Short meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Urgency** | **Quality–Contact Force Relation** | Stakes meeting real-world asks. |
| **Routine** | **Contact–Continuity Force Relation** | Repeated asks becoming regular work. |
| **Craft** | **Quality–Continuity Force Relation** | Stakes held through standards over time. |

---

## 9. Urgency

**Urgency** is the relation between Stakes and Asks.

It appears when something important is also making contact with reality now.

Urgency asks:

```text
What matters and needs contact now?
```

Examples:

- an important email that needs a reply today
- a deadline with real consequences
- a customer issue that affects trust
- a creative opportunity with a time window

Urgency is useful when it clarifies action.

It becomes distorted when everything is treated as urgent.

---

## 10. Routine

**Routine** is the relation between Asks and Standards.

It appears when repeated asks become regular work.

Routine asks:

```text
What repeated ask needs a reliable pattern?
```

Examples:

- weekly scheduling
- bookkeeping
- answering standard inquiries
- posting listings
- preparing recurring materials
- maintaining a workflow

Routine is useful when it reduces decision load.

It becomes distorted when it continues after it no longer serves the work.

---

## 11. Craft

**Craft** is the relation between Stakes and Standards.

It appears when something important needs to be held to a meaningful standard over time.

Craft asks:

```text
What deserves care?
What standard preserves the value of this work?
```

Examples:

- writing that keeps a particular voice
- a public-facing document with durable value
- a process that should be done carefully
- a creative project that needs coherence
- work that expresses taste, judgment, or responsibility

Craft is useful when the work deserves care.

It becomes distorted when everything is treated as a craft object.

---

## 12. Drive

**Drive** is the Working Register phrase for **Force Formation**.

Drive forms when:

- **Stakes** clarify what matters
- **Asks** keep the work in contact with reality
- **Standards** give the work reliable form
- **Urgency**, **Routine**, and **Craft** support each other

Drive is not mere busyness.

Drive is formed work-energy.

It means the work has enough importance, contact, and standard to move coherently.

---

## 13. Bandwidth

**Bandwidth** is the Working Register translation of **The Three Capacities**.

It describes what the person, team, or system can actually bear.

The three forms of Bandwidth are:

| Working term | Canonical Layer 1 source | Short meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Attention** | **Bodily/Regulatory Capacity** | The capacity to focus, care, notice, and stay mentally present. |
| **Load** | **Social/Structural Capacity** | The amount of work, contact, responsibility, and external demand being carried. |
| **Throughput** | **Form/Integrity Capacity** | The capacity to move work through a reliable form or process. |

---

## 14. Attention

**Attention** is the work-facing translation of **Bodily/Regulatory Capacity**.

Attention names how much focus, care, presence, and cognitive-emotional energy are available.

Attention asks:

```text
Do I have the focus for this?
Can I stay with it?
Is this the right kind of work for my current state?
```

Attention is affected by sleep, stress, emotion, interruption, hunger, environment, and task complexity.

Not all tasks require the same kind of attention.

---

## 15. Load

**Load** is the work-facing translation of **Social/Structural Capacity**.

Load names how much the person or system is carrying.

Load may include:

- number of tasks
- number of people involved
- emotional demands
- operational complexity
- interruptions
- deadlines
- financial pressure
- family responsibilities
- unclear expectations

Load asks:

```text
How much am I carrying?
What is adding weight?
What support, structure, or reduction is needed?
```

High load does not always mean failure.

But high load without support will distort the system.

---

## 16. Throughput

**Throughput** is the work-facing translation of **Form/Integrity Capacity**.

Throughput names the system’s ability to move work from intention to completion.

Throughput asks:

```text
Can this work actually move through the system?
Where does it get stuck?
What process, template, boundary, or standard would help?
```

Throughput depends on:

- clear steps
- tools
- templates
- batching
- time blocks
- repeatable workflows
- finishing criteria
- decision rules

A person may have high Stakes and high Attention but poor Throughput.

That often creates frustration.

---

## 17. Bandwidth relations

The three Bandwidth elements combine into three relations.

| Working term | Canonical Layer 1 source | Short meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | **Bodily/Regulatory–Social/Structural Capacity Relation** | Attention supported under load. |
| **Endurance** | **Social/Structural–Form/Integrity Capacity Relation** | Load carried through a reliable structure over time. |
| **Quality** | **Bodily/Regulatory–Form/Integrity Capacity Relation** | Attention held through a coherent standard or form. |

---

## 18. Focus

**Focus** is the relation between Attention and Load.

Focus asks:

```text
Can my attention meet this load?
```

Examples:

- choosing one task instead of ten
- reducing interruptions
- matching task difficulty to current attention
- using a list to lower mental load
- setting a short timer

Focus is not just willpower.

It is attention supported under load.

---

## 19. Endurance

**Endurance** is the relation between Load and Throughput.

Endurance asks:

```text
Can this load move through a reliable structure over time?
```

Examples:

- making a weekly workflow
- batching repeated tasks
- simplifying a recurring process
- assigning roles
- making the work less dependent on mood

Endurance is not pushing forever.

It is load carried through structure.

---

## 20. Quality

**Quality** is the relation between Attention and Throughput.

Quality asks:

```text
Can I preserve care while moving the work through?
```

Examples:

- using a checklist to preserve standards
- editing after drafting
- creating a house style
- doing high-attention work at the right time of day
- defining what “good enough” means

Quality becomes distorted when it turns into perfectionism.

---

## 21. Capacity

**Capacity** is the Working Register phrase for **Capacity Formation**.

Capacity forms when:

- **Attention** is available
- **Load** is bearable
- **Throughput** can move work forward
- **Focus**, **Endurance**, and **Quality** support one another

Capacity is not the same as ambition.

Capacity is what the system can actually bear and move.

---

## 22. Moves

**Moves** are the Working Register translation of **The Three Operations**.

They describe what can be done to tune the work system.

The three Moves are:

| Working term | Canonical Layer 1 source | Short meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Sharpen** | **Explore Operation** | Clarify what matters. |
| **Triage** | **Coordinate Operation** | Decide what needs contact now, later, less, or not at all. |
| **Repattern** | **Stabilize Operation** | Change the workflow, routine, template, boundary, or recurring structure. |

---

## 23. Sharpen

**Sharpen** is the work-facing translation of **Explore Operation**.

Sharpening clarifies what matters.

It asks:

```text
What is the real point?
What is the sharpest version of this task?
What matters most?
What can be made clearer?
```

Examples:

- naming the actual priority
- writing the one-sentence goal
- clarifying the audience
- deciding what this task is really for
- identifying the highest-value part of the work

Sharpening helps when the work is vague, scattered, or emotionally noisy.

---

## 24. Triage

**Triage** is the work-facing translation of **Coordinate Operation**.

Triaging decides what needs contact and when.

It asks:

```text
What needs action now?
What can wait?
What can be reduced?
What can be delegated?
What can be ignored?
What needs a response?
```

Examples:

- choosing the next task
- answering the one email that matters
- deferring low-value work
- cutting the scope
- deciding who needs to be contacted
- matching effort to actual stakes

Triaging is not avoidance.

Good triage is honest contact with reality.

---

## 25. Repattern

**Repattern** is the work-facing translation of **Stabilize Operation**.

Repatterning changes the recurring shape of the work.

It asks:

```text
What pattern needs to change?
What system would make this easier next time?
What template, routine, boundary, or process would help?
```

Examples:

- creating a checklist
- changing the weekly schedule
- making a reusable template
- setting a recurring work block
- changing a communication pattern
- making a better default

Repatterning helps when the same problem keeps returning.

---

## 26. Move relations

The three Moves combine into three relations.

| Working term | Canonical Layer 1 source | Short meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Prioritizing** | **Explore–Coordinate Operation Relation** | Sharpening what matters by bringing it into contact with reality. |
| **Restructuring** | **Coordinate–Stabilize Operation Relation** | Turning contact with reality into a better structure. |
| **Re-skilling** | **Explore–Stabilize Operation Relation** | Turning clarified value into a repeatable capability. |

---

## 27. Adjustment

**Adjustment** is the Working Register phrase for **Operation Formation**.

Adjustment forms when:

- **Sharpening** clarifies what matters
- **Triaging** brings the work into contact with reality
- **Repatterning** changes the structure
- **Prioritizing**, **Restructuring**, and **Re-skilling** support one another

Adjustment means the work system can adapt.

It is not just doing more.

It is changing the relationship between what matters, what is being asked, and what can be sustained.

---

## 28. Diagnostic translation

The canonical diagnostic overlay uses:

- **Slack**
- **Tight**
- **Misdirected**
- **Coherent Formation**

In the Working Register, these can be translated as:

| Canonical term | Working Register translation |
|---|---|
| **Slack** | Underpowered, unsupported, vague, insufficiently structured, not enough contact. |
| **Tight** | Over-pressured, over-controlled, rigid, perfectionistic, over-scheduled. |
| **Misdirected** | Working on the wrong thing, solving the wrong problem, preserving the wrong pattern. |
| **Coherent Formation** | The work has enough stakes, support, structure, and movement to proceed. |

Important:

**Coherent Formation** is not a fourth distortion.

It is the well-formed condition.

---

## 29. Slack in the Working Register

**Slack** means the work is underpowered, under-supported, vague, or insufficiently structured.

Examples:

- unclear priority
- no deadline or contact point
- too little attention
- too little support
- no process
- no next action
- vague intention without structure

Plain question:

```text
Where is there not enough force, support, contact, or structure?
```

Possible move:

```text
Add one clear stake, one contact point, or one small structure.
```

---

## 30. Tight in the Working Register

**Tight** means the work is over-pressured, over-controlled, rigid, perfectionistic, or overloaded.

Examples:

- treating everything as urgent
- too many tasks in one day
- perfectionism blocking completion
- overplanning instead of acting
- rigid standards for low-stakes work
- no room for interruption or reality

Plain question:

```text
Where is there too much pressure, control, or rigidity?
```

Possible move:

```text
Reduce scope, lower the standard, create more room, or define “good enough.”
```

---

## 31. Misdirected in the Working Register

**Misdirected** means real effort is aimed at the wrong thing.

Examples:

- polishing work that does not matter
- organizing instead of doing the difficult contact point
- answering low-stakes emails instead of the important one
- building a system for a task that should be deleted
- preserving a workflow that no longer serves the work

Plain question:

```text
Am I solving the right problem?
```

Possible move:

```text
Return to the real stakes, real ask, and real next contact point.
```

---

## 32. Coherent Formation in the Working Register

**Coherent Formation** means the work system is formed enough to proceed.

In Working Register language:

```text
The stakes are clear enough.
The asks are in contact with reality.
The standards are appropriate.
The bandwidth is sufficient enough.
The next move is visible.
```

This does not mean the work is complete.

It means the system is workable.

---

## 33. Practical check-in

A simple Working Register check-in:

```text
1. What are the stakes?
2. What is actually being asked?
3. What standard is appropriate?
4. What bandwidth is available?
5. What move is needed: Sharpen, Triage, or Repattern?
6. Is anything Slack, Tight, or Misdirected?
7. What would make the work formed enough to proceed?
```

A shorter version:

```text
What matters?
What is being asked?
What can I actually handle?
What move would help?
```

## 34. Example use

### Situation

```text
I have a huge to-do list and I feel behind before the day has started.
```

### Working Register reading

- **Stakes:** Not every task matters equally.
- **Asks:** Some tasks need real contact today; others may only be mental noise.
- **Standards:** Some work needs care, but some only needs completion.
- **Attention:** Limited.
- **Load:** Too high.
- **Throughput:** At risk because the list is too undifferentiated.
- **Likely distortion:** Tight Load and Slack Triage.
- **Helpful Move:** Triage, then Sharpen.

### Plain next move

```text
Choose the three tasks that actually matter today.
Define “good enough” for each.
Start with the one that needs real contact first.
```

---

## 35. Example use: project sprawl

### Situation

```text
A project keeps expanding. Every new idea seems useful, but nothing is getting finished.
```

### Working Register reading

- **Stakes:** The project matters and has genuine creative value.
- **Asks:** The real ask may be smaller than the expanding idea-field.
- **Standards:** The standard may be unclear or too high for the current stage.
- **Attention:** Attention is being pulled toward novelty.
- **Load:** The project is carrying too many possibilities at once.
- **Throughput:** Work is not moving cleanly toward completion.
- **Likely distortion:** Tight Stakes, Slack Throughput, and Misdirected Sharpening.
- **Helpful Move:** Triage scope, then Repattern the project into phases.

### Plain next move

```text
Name the smallest finished version.
Move future ideas into a parking lot.
Finish the next visible unit.
```

---

## 36. Caution

The Working Register should not become productivity moralism.

Avoid implying that a person’s worth depends on output.

Avoid treating all stuckness as laziness.

The Working Register should help locate the actual pattern:

- unclear stakes
- too many asks
- unrealistic standards
- low attention
- excessive load
- poor throughput
- wrong move
- distorted structure

The goal is not to do more.

The goal is to make the work system more coherent.

## 38. Working rule

When using the Working Register, ask:

```text
What matters?
What is being asked?
What can I actually handle?
What move would help?
```

If the answer becomes too abstract, return to the work itself and choose one useful next action.
